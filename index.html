1. Subatomic Particles

Atoms consist of three main subatomic particles:

  • Protons (+) Mass ≈ 1 amu | Charge = +1
  • Neutrons (0) Mass ≈ 1 amu | Charge = 0
  • Electrons (-) Mass ≈ 0.0005 amu | Charge = -1

2. What are Isotopes?

Isotopes are variants of the same chemical element that have the same number of protons (Z) but a different number of neutrons (N).

Example: Carbon Isotopes
• Carbon-12: 6 Protons + 6 Neutrons (Stable, 98.9%)
• Carbon-14: 6 Protons + 8 Neutrons (Radioactive, used for dating)

3. What are Ions?

An Ion is an atom with a net electric charge due to the loss or gain of one or more electrons.

Cation (+)
Lost electrons → Net Positive Charge (e.g., Na⁺)
Anion (-)
Gained electrons → Net Negative Charge (e.g., Cl⁻)

4. Nuclear Stability

The strong nuclear force holds protons and neutrons together in the nucleus. Light elements require roughly a 1:1 neutron-to-proton ratio for stability. If an atom has too many or too few neutrons, it becomes unstable and decays.
